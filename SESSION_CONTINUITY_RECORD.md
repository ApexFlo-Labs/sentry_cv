## 1. Purpose

This file preserves the detailed working context behind the current Sentinel CV
runtime. It is a historical handoff, not a wire contract. The normative inputs
and outputs are under `newdetails/sentry-v6/`; the edge-side event-production
rules are in `SENTINEL_V5_2_PRODUCTION_POLICY.md`.

The long design discussion repeatedly separated two concerns:

- The contract defines what Management sends to edge and what edge may send
  back.
- The production policy defines how edge collects, confirms, associates,
  deduplicates, selects, and emits trustworthy observations.

Do not put detector internals such as tracker distance, stable-hit count, face
pixel thresholds, or OCR candidate scoring into Management desired state unless
there is a deliberate future contract decision to expose bounded policy knobs.

## 3. Architectural Decisions That Persisted

### 3.1 Edge versus Management

Edge owns physical observations:

- Decode and camera reconnect.
- Detection and camera-local tracking.
- Zone/line geometry evaluation.
- Camera-local `presence_id` continuity.
- ANPR candidate collection and OCR outcome.
- Face, body, gait, and scene embeddings.
- Exact source-frame evidence selection.
- Quality gates, temporal confirmation, deduplication, and durable delivery.

Management owns semantic and business interpretation:

- Cross-camera identity and named face recognition.
- Vector indexing and text-to-image semantic search.
- Rules, schedules, incidents, alerts, escalation, and operator workflows.
- Dwell/business thresholds and cross-camera lifecycle reconstruction.

`track_id` is temporary and camera/session-local. `presence_id` can survive a
qualified local track break but is still camera/session-local. It is never a
global person or vehicle identity.

### 3.2 Detection Is Not An Event

Per-frame detections remain internal. An output is emitted only after its own
collection, confirmation, association, deduplication, and emission gates pass.
Outputs were conceptually divided into:

- Durable facts such as zone entry, line crossing, stable plate read, and
  camera unavailable.
- Current measurements such as `visible_now` counts.
- Supporting artifacts such as crops, context frames, and embeddings.

### 3.3 Shared Presence State

Zone state, dwell continuity, ANPR, face, body, gait, and event production use
one camera-session presence registry. Track loss is not a zone exit. A presence
becomes temporarily unobserved, may be recovered after an ambiguity-aware
association, or eventually ends unknown. Only a positive outside observation or
validated exit transition is a real exit.

Recovery must consider time, location, motion, class, size, appearance, plate
text when available, and conflicting active candidates. A nearby new track is
not automatically the same presence.

## 4. Contract Evolution

### 4.1 V18.1

V18.1 established multimodal person Re-ID and full-frame scene search. It used
YOLO person/vehicle detection, camera-local tracking, SCRFD/AdaFace for face,
TransReID for body, OpenCV silhouette generation plus GaitBase for gait, and
SigLIP2 for scene embeddings. The gait silhouette is generated by a technique
(MOG2 plus morphology); GaitBase is the model that converts a qualified
silhouette sequence into an embedding.

V18.1 also contained stronger gates in selected paths, including line-crossing
deadband/movement/cooldown and face best-candidate/dedup behavior. Later work
identified that V4 producers did not consistently retain those protections.

### 4.2 V4 And V5 Analysis

The V4 discussion exposed several gaps:

- Events, evidence, and embeddings could exist without one shared production
  policy.
- Presence lifecycle telemetry could crowd out business/use-case events.
- Zone-to-use-case binding was unclear.
- Scene samples were full-frame with `zone_id=null`; polygon crops were a
  possible future path, not tested behavior.
- Periodic scene sampling alone cannot prove temporal actions such as touching,
  fighting, falling, or picking up an object.

The desired geometry model became: define polygons/lines once and bind zero,
one, or many use-case instances to them. A use case may also be full-frame when
geometry is not required. Do not duplicate one polygon merely because counting,
presence, dwell, or scene context reuse it.

The V5 contract remained the base while stale fields and overly exposed
edge-internal gates were removed. Face and body were not to be jointly
mandatory: face can be unavailable while body remains useful. Face, body, and
gait are separate modality samples correlated to the same local presence.

### 4.3 V5.2

V5.2 clarified:

- Flexible full-frame or geometry bindings.
- Presence, zone episode, evidence, and embedding relationships.
- Scene events and full-frame JPEG evidence.
- Plate outcomes and evidence without requiring a separate plate-crop contract
  artifact when the agreed evidence is a contextual frame/vehicle view plus
  plate bbox metadata.
- Recheck was intentionally deferred/removed from the immediate desired-state
  path after discussion.
- `face_crop` and `face_chip` ambiguity was removed. The aligned model input and
  the wider human-readable evidence crop have different internal purposes, but
  the contract should not expose confusing duplicate artifacts.

### 4.4 V6

The current image consumes the V6 contract in `newdetails/sentry-v6/` while
using the latest V5.2 production policy for edge-side decisions. The contract
does not replace the production policy, and the production policy does not
change V6 wire payloads unless an explicit schema update is made.

## 5. Current Use-Case Semantics

### 5.1 Person And Vehicle Detection/Presence

YOLO detections enter tracking at the collection threshold. A stable local
presence emits `object_present` once on confirmation, then a sparse freshness
update rather than one event per frame. The present production-policy default
is a 60-second freshness update while still present.

Scope may be full-frame or geometry. First seeing a subject already inside a
zone starts an inside episode with unknown entry; it must not invent a boundary
crossing. Presence evidence is selected from the original decoded source frame,
not a resized detector tensor. Bbox and applicable zone metadata remain in the
event.

### 5.2 Counts

Counts are `visible_now`, not cumulative arrivals. They are calculated from
qualifying current tracks, for a bound zone when present or the full frame when
no zone is bound. Edge emits after a stable value change and may send a slower
heartbeat. A healthy `count=0` is valid, but camera/inference failure must
produce unknown/unavailable coverage rather than a false zero. Stale tracker
predictions must not inflate current visible counts.

Cumulative entry/exit totals belong to line-crossing/transition events, not
`zone_count`.

### 5.3 Zone Entry, Exit, And Dwell

Zone entry/exit uses the canonical presence and positive geometry transitions,
with boundary margin and repeated observations. Track disappearance never
becomes exit.

Dwell begins from the first confirmed inside observation for a zone episode.
Management can derive elapsed time from occurrence timestamps, episode ID,
last-confirmed-inside updates, recovery gaps, and confirmed exit. Sparse
milestones/status updates may be sent; per-frame dwell events are not sent. An
unobserved gap is uncertainty and does not automatically reset or complete
dwell.

All event and evidence timestamps must describe the source observation time,
not processing completion or Management receipt time.

### 5.4 Line Crossing

Line crossing requires a stable presence, deadband handling, minimum movement,
actual segment intersection, direction, crossing state, and cooldown. Side
jitter must not produce repeated cross/uncross events. A line crossing and a
zone exit are independent facts.

### 5.5 Person Identity And Re-ID

Person identity uses three independent modalities connected to the same local
presence and identity sample group:

| Modality | Implementation | Current output |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Face | AdaFace IR101 INT8 | 512D L2-normalized |
| Body Re-ID | TransReID-SSL INT8 | 384D L2-normalized |
| Gait | GaitBase INT8 over qualified silhouettes | 4096D L2-normalized |

The body model profile is `transreid-ssl-v18.1`, with `256x128` RGB input scaled
to `[-1,1]`, on NPU. Current code and contract both require 384 dimensions; old
notes mentioning a 768D body vector are not current runtime truth.

The face and body candidates are cropped independently from the original source
frame. The human-readable face evidence includes extra context pixels; the
aligned 112x112 face input is internal model provenance. Gait waits for a usable
moving silhouette sequence. The edge sends the first qualified best sample and
only meaningful improvements, not continuous embeddings for every frame or for
every minute a person remains visible.

Cross-camera matching happens in Management by comparing compatible embedding
profiles and using camera/time topology. The three modalities must not be
concatenated blindly or compared across different embedding spaces.

### 5.6 ANPR

ANPR starts from a vehicle presence. The vehicle crop is taken from the original
source frame; plate detection runs within it, and the plate crop for OCR is also
mapped back to original-frame pixels.

Earlier permissive changes removed the India-only regex and four-identical-read
requirement. OCR minimum confidence was discussed and set to `0.25` for
collection. The production direction is a bounded best-candidate window:

- Collect usable plate detections and OCR attempts.
- Normalize text for comparison without hiding uncertain characters.
- Keep raw candidates and their confidence/quality.
- Prefer a later materially better, clearer candidate.
- Emit one stable `plate_read` when accepted.
- Emit an explicit unreadable/failed outcome if no reliable text is obtained.
- Corrections must refer to the earlier accepted result.

Collection thresholds are not final truth thresholds. Avoid emitting the first
weak OCR result merely because one read exceeded 0.25. Candidate limits should
consider elapsed time, useful observations, attempts, plate pixels, sharpness,
and agreement. ANPR and tracking/adaptive tuning were explicitly left for a
later focused pass after the V6 and production-policy alignment.

### 5.7 Scene Search

Scene processing branches directly from the decoded full frame and is independent
of YOLO/tracking:

```text
decoded frame -> scene sampler -> JPEG evidence -> SigLIP2 -> 768D embedding
```

The current scene embedding is 768D and L2-normalized. Management creates the
compatible text vector and performs semantic search. A scene sample is search
evidence, not proof of a temporal action.

The normal active interval was changed from roughly 10/60 seconds in older
drafts to 2 seconds. One-second sampling may be considered for selected cameras
only after load testing. Event-triggered samples can later supplement periodic
sampling, with cooldown/coalescing so the same frame is not duplicated.

Polygon scene processing means crop or mask the configured visual region before
SigLIP. Reusing a zone for YOLO logic does not automatically make the scene
embedding zone-scoped. Full-frame samples use no zone ID.

### 5.8 Fire And Smoke

Hazard output uses repeated positive detections and confidence/coverage gates to
open a suspected episode, then repeated usable negative observations to clear
it. Missing frames, disabled inference, or camera failure are not confirmed
negative observations. Management decides whether a suspected model observation
becomes an incident or alert.

### 5.9 Health And Delivery

Health is transition-oriented: healthy, degraded, unavailable, and recovered,
plus slower heartbeat. A live HTTP server is not sufficient readiness; worker,
revision, camera, models, and processing state must initialize.

Durable delivery persists event JSON, evidence bytes, embedding bytes, IDs, and
hashes before first send. Logical order is event, evidence, then embedding.
Partial acknowledgement must be tracked per component so accepted events cannot
cause promised evidence to be discarded. Retries reuse identical IDs and bytes.

The earlier Management report of `ready=false`, `worker_running=false`, revision
0, and zero cameras was a worker-startup/config/dependency problem, not evidence
that V5.2 schemas alone were incompatible. Startup exceptions must be visible in
logs, and readiness must not be weakened to hide initialization failure.

## 6. Adaptive Production Policy

The agreed policy direction is bounded adaptation per camera and use case, not
one global strict/tolerant switch. Input quality and pipeline performance are
measured separately.

Useful measurements include resolution, exposure, blur, object/plate pixel
size, occlusion, scene density, detection dropout, track fragmentation, OCR
stability, queue delay, and outbox pressure. Track fragmentation may indicate a
tracker/FPS/latency problem and must not automatically be called poor camera
quality.

Adaptation may collect longer, retain recovery state longer, or request more
candidates. It must not silently lower the final acceptance standard. Resolved
values, reasons, and changes need telemetry and hysteresis. Recorded footage
must compare adaptive behavior with a fixed baseline before claiming an
improvement.

Common historical defaults documented in the policy include detector collection
confidence 0.30, dedup IoU 0.55, containment 0.85, tracker maximum distance 320
pixels, maximum disappeared 45 processed frames, bbox smoothing 0.25, minimum
IoU 0.01, stable presence 3 hits, and candidate maximum gap 1.5 seconds. These
are starting values, not universal production-optimal constants. Distance and
retention should become motion-, time-, FPS-, and resolution-aware. Predicted
boxes never count as fresh real detections.

## 7. Motion Gate Integration

Motion gating was ported from the separate dev11 experiment into V6 dev13 as a
built-in CV behavior. It is not selected through Management desired state.

Production/default path:

- `SENTINEL_MOTION_GATE_ENABLED=true`.
- BGR/OpenCV motion gate.
- Starts in active `TRACKING` state.
- Releases to idle `MOTION` after 6 consecutive fully evaluated frames with no
  vehicle and no fire/smoke detections.
- At configured 5 FPS, six frames are nominally 1.2 seconds, although heavy
  inference can make wall-clock time longer.
- Idle mode evaluates optical flow on decoded frames.
- Wake requires at least 4 motion-positive frames among the latest 14.
- Forced full-inference sweep occurs every 5 seconds.
- Feature re-detection occurs every 25 frames.
- Current optical-flow constants are `MAG_TH=0.2`, `MEANMAG_TH=0.08`,
  `MOVEPCT_TH=2.0`, and `CONS_TH=0.42`.

Quiet processing must not fabricate `count=0`, zone exit, presence end, or fire
clear. During active inference, scene sampling remains approximately every 2
seconds. During idle motion mode, scene sampling currently rides on the forced
five-second inference sweep and is therefore approximately every 5 seconds.

An experimental native VA-surface selective-conversion path is included but is
disabled with `SENTINEL_SURFACE_MOTION_GATE_ENABLED=false`; it was unstable and
must not be described as production-ready. The production BGR path supports the
tested 2560x1440 stream, but adaptive motion thresholds remain a future task.

### 10.1 Face Pose And Identity Fragmentation Assessment

On 2026-10-21, the Management People view showed many separate Unknown records
whose thumbnails appeared to include repeated people, with several profile,
downward-facing, and otherwise weak face views. The UI thumbnails are wider
evidence crops; AdaFace receives a separately aligned 112x112 chip, so thumbnail
appearance alone is not a complete embedding-quality measurement. Nevertheless,
the current edge quality score uses detector confidence, face width, aligned-chip
sharpness, and exposure but has no explicit yaw, pitch, or roll term. Its default
minimum quality is 0.20 and its best-candidate window is 1.5 seconds.

`PIPELINE/SixDRepNet/weights` contains `best_a0.pt` (15,850,443 bytes),
`best_a1.pt` (25,829,003 bytes), and a separate face detector ONNX file. The PT
files are serialized PyTorch RepVGG models, but that folder contains no model
source, preprocessing definition, provenance record, or license. They are not
drop-in models for the current OpenVINO-only image and must be traced, exported,
validated, and benchmarked before use.

The following direction was implemented for the V6 dev14 build:

- Use head pose only as an internal face-candidate gate/ranking component.
- Run the validated RepVGG-A0 FP16 OpenVINO model after SCRFD and before AdaFace.
- Compile directly on NPU with FP16 inference precision and no CPU fallback.
- Rank candidates continuously by pose quality; do not rely only on one hard
  yaw/pitch/roll cutoff.
- Require a recognition-grade pose for a face sample used in named recognition.
  If none appears, omit face and retain body/gait samples for candidate
  association rather than sending a weak face vector.
- Extend candidate selection from 1.5 seconds to 4 seconds so a later frontal
  view can replace an early profile view.
- Keep head-pose thresholds CV-owned under the production policy. V6 does not
  require a contract change if only the selected aggregate `quality` and sample
  are emitted. A future explainability requirement for yaw/pitch/roll would need
  an explicit schema revision because current schemas reject extra fields.

Implemented defaults are maximum absolute pitch 25 degrees, yaw 35 degrees, and
roll 25 degrees. The original decoded-frame face region receives 20 percent
context before RGB 224x224 ImageNet preprocessing. Pose-failing samples are
suppressed before AdaFace. The existing visual quality is combined with the pose
quality, and the default final face quality floor increased from 0.20 to 0.35.
Angles remain internal and V6 schemas are unchanged.

On a deterministic 400-image sample of the supplied surveillance face set, 149
samples (37.25 percent) passed these recognition-grade limits. Yaw was the main
rejection reason. All four NPU models (SixDRepNet, AdaFace, TransReID, and
GaitBase) compiled and produced finite outputs concurrently on the target host.

### 10.2 Adaptive Face Lifecycle

The V6 dev15 change makes face-model scheduling adaptive per local track while
keeping the recognition-quality thresholds fixed and CV-owned. New tracks run
the face path every 0.6 seconds for 10 seconds, then back off to 1.5 seconds
until 30 seconds and 5 seconds thereafter if no qualified face is found. The
four-second best-candidate window remains full-rate. After acquisition, checks
run every 3 seconds, or every 15 seconds when the selected quality is at least
0.75. A real-observation gap of at least two seconds starts a four-second fast
recovery window. Scheduler state expires after 20 seconds away to avoid a
reused tracker ID inheriting another subject's face baseline.

Tracker-predicted boxes are excluded from face inference. SCRFD, SixDRepNet,
and AdaFace continue to receive pixels derived from `packet.frame`, which is the
native decoded frame; detector coordinates are mapped back into that frame.
The SixDRepNet crop expands the original face box by 20 percent on the matching
axis. Management evidence remains a square original-frame crop with twice the
largest face dimension, while AdaFace receives its separate landmark-aligned
112x112 chip.

The scheduler never lowers SCRFD confidence, minimum pixels, pose limits, final
quality, embedding validity, or the 0.10 material-improvement requirement. A
one-hour unit simulation at five FPS with a stable 0.80-quality face reduced
scheduled face-model attempts from approximately 6,000 to fewer than 260 and
forwarded fewer than 10 candidates into V6 selection.

### 10.3 Per-Camera Runtime Telemetry

The V6 dev16 change adds detailed operational telemetry to the original
`GET /healthz` route without changing liveness semantics. There is no health
alias. The route continues to return HTTP 200 while the API process is alive;
the JSON fields `ready` and `worker_running` describe worker readiness.
`GET /readyz` retains its existing probe behavior. Each camera entry also
contains its health state, reason, last reliable time, status time, and whether
its supervisor is retrying.

Each camera process atomically publishes a two-second telemetry snapshot under
`/state/metrics`. The health response exposes target, decoded, inference,
motion-gate quiet, and durable-event output FPS separately. This distinction is
intentional: decoded frames can continue while the motion gate suppresses
model inference, and an actively processed camera can correctly produce zero
durable events in a window. Output rates are grouped by event type and by V6
`use_case_id`, with the corresponding app name.

Timed stages include vehicle/person detection, plate detection and OCR,
tracking/geometry, presence/zone lifecycle, line crossing, counting, ANPR
policy, fire/smoke detection and policy, face detection, SixDRepNet pose,
AdaFace, body, gait, scene embedding, event policy, and durable persistence.
The endpoint also reports source-observation-to-outbox-persistence latency by
event type, so deliberate face/ANPR candidate windows remain visible rather
than being mistaken for transport latency.

Resource reporting includes host CPU/RAM/swap, fleet-worker RSS/threads/file
descriptors, and per-camera process CPU/RSS/thread counts. Model device
placement is explicit. Timed GPU/NPU model workload is reported in
milliseconds per second. It is not labelled as hardware utilization: when the
driver exposes no supported busy counter, `hardware_utilization_pct` is null
with `counter_unavailable`. Prometheus equivalents are exported by `/metrics`.

### 10.4 Corrected Resource And Stage Telemetry

The V6 dev17 change corrects the interpretation and collection problems found
during the Kubernetes-shaped dev16 audit. Fire/smoke once again follows its
configured five-frame cadence during active processing; an idle motion-gate
sweep still forces a fire/smoke evaluation so a static hazard is not hidden.
Skipped periodic fire/smoke checks preserve prior confirmed-negative motion
gate progress instead of resetting it. Plate detector latency is now recorded
only when a vehicle crop actually invokes the model.

The fire/smoke model also changes from OpenVINO `THROUGHPUT` to `LATENCY`.
An isolated GPU benchmark showed that the old synchronous call selected an
optimal request count of 1024 and averaged about 1004 ms, while `LATENCY`
selected one request and averaged about 1.41 ms. This model is invoked
synchronously at a bounded cadence, so latency mode matches the actual use.

Every timed stage reports invocation count and calls per second. Windows with
no invocation return null recent latency instead of presenting a lifetime
average as a current measurement. Parent pipeline spans are marked with
`includes_child_stages=true`, so Management must not sum a parent with its
children. A complete frame-processing span is also published.

Camera CPU is reported as cores consumed, avoiding the ambiguous psutil
one-core percentage scale. RSS, PSS and USS are labelled as camera-process
measurements. Container CPU and memory come

from the node's `npu_busy_time_us` delta. GPU utilization remains null because
the installed `intel_gpu_top` is incompatible with this driver and no supported

## 11. Anti-Regression Checklist

Before changing the runtime, preserve these decisions:

- Do not equate track loss with exit.
- Do not make `presence_id` global across cameras.
- Do not emit model output every frame.
- Do not require a visible face before body Re-ID can be useful.
- Do not send face/body/gait as one undifferentiated vector.
- Do not report a camera failure as a healthy zero count.
- Do not calculate event time from upload/ingest time.
- Do not promise image evidence unless its durable delivery state is retained.
- Do not accept the first weak OCR string as the best plate result.
- Do not claim scene embeddings prove temporal actions.
- Do not make the motion gate a Management-configured V6 use case.
- Do not enable the unstable native VA-surface gate by default.
- Do not push to ApexFlo-Labs unless explicitly requested.
